# Press & Media Outreach Plan — PickNKick

## The Pitch Angle

Journalists don't write about apps. They write about **stories**. Your story is:

> "Pickup football groups worldwide waste 10-15 minutes arguing about teams before every game. A developer who plays weekly built a free tool that turns team selection into a live snake draft on everyone's phones — and it's spreading through WhatsApp groups."

**Why this is a story:**
- Relatable problem (anyone who's played pickup sports knows it)
- Simple, clever solution (snake draft = fair, no arguments)
- Viral mechanic (teams shared via WhatsApp → link back to app)
- Free, no VC, no ads — just a player solving his own problem
- Works globally (English-first, web-based, no app store)

---

## Press Kit — Prepare Before Outreach

Create a folder at `https://picknkick.com/press/` (static HTML page in `public/press/`) or a Google Drive folder with:

1. **One-pager** (PDF or page)
   - What PickNKick is (2 sentences)
   - The problem it solves
   - How it works (4 bullet points)
   - Key stats if you have them (users, drafts run, countries)
   - Your name and contact
   - Link to app

2. **Screenshots** (high-res PNG, no browser chrome)
   - Landing page (mobile)
   - Draft board mid-draft (mobile)
   - Results page with teams (mobile)
   - WhatsApp share preview

3. **Logo** (PNG with transparent background, SVG if available)
   - Dark background version
   - Light background version

4. **Short demo video** (30-60 seconds)
   - Screen recording of the full flow: select players → draft → results → WhatsApp share
   - No narration needed, just the app in action
   - Upload to YouTube as unlisted, include link in emails

5. **Founder photo** (optional but helps — journalists like putting a face to a story)

## Tier 1 — Sports Tech & Innovation Sites

These cover the intersection of sports and technology. Most likely to write about you.

### Product Hunt
**URL:** https://www.producthunt.com
**What:** Launch platform for new products. Big visibility spike on launch day.
**How to submit:** Create a maker account, schedule a launch
**Tagline:** "Fair teams. No arguments. — Live snake draft for pickup football"
**Tips:**
- Launch on a Tuesday or Wednesday
- Prepare 4-5 screenshots + a GIF
- Write a clear "maker comment" explaining why you built it
- Ask your football group to upvote (genuinely, not spammy)
- This can drive hundreds of signups in one day

### Hacker News (Show HN)
**URL:** https://news.ycombinator.com
**Format:** "Show HN: PickNKick — Live snake draft for pickup football teams"
**Tips:**
- Technical audience — lead with the problem and solution
- Be ready to discuss architecture decisions in comments
- If it hits front page, expect thousands of visits
- Post between 8-10am EST on weekday

### BetaList
**URL:** https://betalist.com
**What:** Directory of new startups/products
**How:** Submit via their form, free tier available
**Good for:** SEO backlink + steady trickle of signups

### SideProjectors
**URL:** https://www.sideprojectors.com
**What:** Showcase for side projects
**Low effort, good for backlink and visibility**

## Outreach Strategy

### Phase 1: Build credibility (Week 1-2)
- Post on Reddit (r/SideProject, r/5aside)
- Post on Indie Hackers, DEV.to, Product Hunt
- Write a Medium/DEV.to technical article
- Collect early user feedback and fix issues

### Phase 2: Pitch media (Week 3-4)
- Email grassroots football blogs and small sports sites
- DM football content creators on Twitter/Instagram
- Contact local newspapers
- Pitch 2-3 football podcasts

### Phase 3: Scale up (Month 2+)
- If you have traction numbers, pitch bigger outlets (FourFourTwo, Copa90)
- Write a "how it's going" follow-up on Reddit / Indie Hackers
- Reach out to university intramural programs
- Contact 5-a-side facility chains (they could promote to their customers)

---

## Key Rules

1. **Personalize every email** — generic blasts go to spam
2. **Reference their work** — show you've actually read their content
3. **Lead with the story, not the product** — "pickup teams argue" is interesting, "I made an app" is not
4. **Keep it short** — journalists get hundreds of emails. Under 150 words for the pitch.
5. **Follow up once** — if no reply after 5-7 days, one short follow-up is fine. Then move on.
6. **Have your press kit ready** — when someone says "sure, send me screenshots", you need to respond in minutes, not days
7. **Track everything** — spreadsheet with: outlet, contact, date emailed, response, status

---

## Success Metrics

- **Press mentions** — even small blogs help with SEO and credibility
- **Backlinks** — every article linking to picknkick.com improves Google ranking
- **Signups spike** — track new users after each piece publishes
- **WhatsApp shares** — the real viral metric (teams shared → friends visit → sign up)
- **Inbound inquiries** — if writers start coming to you, you've made it
