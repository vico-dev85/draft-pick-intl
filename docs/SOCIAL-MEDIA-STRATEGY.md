# PickNKick — Social Media Strategy

> Last updated: February 2026

---

## What This Page Is

**A page for people who play pickup football.** Not a page for an app.

We're building a community around the culture of casual football — the weekly game, the group chat, the arguments, the banter, the guy in full kit. PickNKick happens to be the thing behind it, but the page is about **the lifestyle**, not the product.

If someone follows us and never downloads the app, that's fine. They followed because the content is about their life. The app sells itself when the time is right.

---

## Who We're Talking To

**Anyone who plays pickup football.** Organizers, regulars, the guy who shows up once and ghosts, the one in full kit, the keeper who doesn't want to be keeper.

- Age 18-40, mostly male, plays weekly or biweekly
- Lives on WhatsApp, scrolls Instagram/TikTok daily
- Follows football culture pages, meme accounts, highlights
- Doesn't follow "sports apps" — follows people and culture
- Speaks in banter, group chat energy, football slang

## Brand Voice

### Identity
We're **the pickup football page**, not an app page. Think of us like a mate who plays every week and happens to make content about it. We just also built an app.

### Personality
- 80% pickup football culture, 20% product
- Cheeky but not try-hard. Observational humor. "You know the guy who..." energy.
- We sound like a group chat, not a brand.

### Do's
- Talk about real situations everyone who plays pickup recognizes
- Use football language: "pitch", "kickoff", "squad", "kit", "boots", "bibs"
- Short, punchy sentences. Group-chat rhythm.
- Make people tag their mates or send to their group
- Swear lightly if it fits (damn, hell yeah) — but never forced

### Don'ts
- Don't sound like an app company
- Don't make every post about PickNKick
- Don't use corporate language ("leverage", "optimize", "solution")
- Don't overuse emojis — 1-2 per caption max
- Don't beg for engagement ("LIKE if you agree!! TAG a friend!!")
- Don't post generic motivational quotes

### Tone Examples

**Good:**
> The guy who says "I'll play anywhere" and then complains about playing left back for 45 minutes straight.

**Bad:**
> Tired of unfair team selection? Try our innovative draft solution! Download now!

**Good:**
> 5-a-side rules that nobody agreed to but everyone follows: last goal wins, no slide tackles, the post doesn't count.

**Bad:**
> PickNKick ensures fair and balanced team distribution through our proprietary snake draft algorithm!

## Content Pillars

### 1. Pickup Football Life (60% of content)

The core of the page. Relatable, funny, shareable content about what it's like to play casual football every week. Zero product mention. This is what builds the audience.

**Themes:**
- **The characters** — every group has them
  - The organizer who does everything and gets no credit
  - The full-kit guy at a casual kickabout
  - The one who's "5 minutes away" for 20 minutes
  - The one who plays like it's a Champions League final
  - The keeper who didn't want to be keeper
  - The one who only shows up when it's sunny
  - The one who brings cones and bibs (legend)
  - The retired pro who hasn't played in 6 months but still nutmegs everyone

- **The moments** — universal pickup experiences
  - Last goal wins and it goes on for 30 minutes
  - Someone calling offside in a game with no ref
  - The post-game WhatsApp analysis that's longer than the actual match
  - When it's 4v4 and someone cancels last minute
  - Playing with one flat ball because nobody brought a pump
  - The first game after winter — everyone's dead after 10 minutes

- **The debates** — engagement magnets
  - 5-a-side vs 7-a-side
  - Winner stays on vs round robin
  - Concrete vs grass vs turf
  - The post counts vs the post doesn't count
  - Are slide tackles ever ok in pickup?

- **The unwritten rules** — everyone knows them, nobody wrote them down
  - Winner stays on
  - No two-touch in goal
  - Last goal wins
  - You score, you celebrate, but not too much
  - The organizer gets first pick of which side to play

- **Tips and hacks** — actually useful content
  - How to organize a weekly game that doesn't fall apart
  - Best way to handle cancellations
  - How to make fair teams (natural lead-in to the app when relevant)
  - What to do when you have an odd number of players
  - How to deal with the guy who always wants to be captain

### 2. The Teams Problem (25% of content)

The specific pain point we solve — picking teams. Still relatable and funny, but zooms in on the one thing PickNKick fixes. Product mention optional, never forced.

**Themes:**
- Arguing about teams for 20 minutes, then playing for 30
- "The teams are fair" (narrator: the teams were not fair)
- The fake WhatsApp argument about who picks teams
- When "random" teams somehow stack all the talent on one side
- The captain who picks all his mates
- Everyone volunteering someone else to be captain
- "We'll sort teams when we get there" → chaos at the pitch

### 3. PickNKick (15% of content)

The actual product. Only show it when it makes sense — usually as a punchline to a "teams problem" post, or as a standalone feature demo. Never more than 1 in 6 posts.

**Themes:**
- How it works (carousel or screen recording)
- Screen recording of a live draft
- "From argument to fair teams in 2 minutes"
- Feature highlights: Quick Draft, Game Night, snake draft
- Before/after: chaos in the parking lot vs clean app results
- User stories and reshares (when we have them)

## Platforms

### Instagram (Primary)

**Why:** Visual, carousels and Reels perform well, the target demo is here, pickup football meme culture already exists.

**Handle:** @picknkick

**Bio:**
> A real-life draft. On your phone. Before the game.
> Stop wasting 20 minutes arguing about teams.
> Fair teams. No arguments. More football.
> picknkick.com

**Posting frequency:** 3x/week feed posts, daily Stories when possible

**Format priority:**
1. Reels (15-30s) — highest reach, algorithm loves them
2. Carousels — highest saves/shares, great for lists and debates
3. Static posts — memes, quotes, one-liners
4. Stories — polls, this-or-that, behind-the-scenes, reshares

---

### TikTok (Secondary, highest upside)

**Why:** Organic reach is unmatched. Football culture content does well. One viral video can do more than a month of Instagram posts.

**Handle:** @picknkick

**Posting frequency:** 2-3x/week (repurpose Reels + native content)

**What works:**
- POV format: "POV: you're the one who organizes the game every week"
- "Types of..." format: "Types of players in every pickup group"
- Skits showing pickup football situations
- Trending sounds + football context
- First 1-2 seconds must hook. Raw > polished.

---

### X / Twitter (Tertiary, low effort)

**Why:** Football Twitter is massive. One-liners get retweeted. Low effort, potentially high reach.

**Handle:** @picknkick

**Posting frequency:** 3-5x/week (mostly text, takes 2 minutes)

**Style:** Observations and hot takes about pickup football.

**Examples:**
> every pickup group has that one guy who hasn't played in months but shows up and runs the game

> there's no greater power than being the one who picks teams

> "yeah the teams are fair" — guy who put himself on the team with the 3 best players

> the 10 minutes after the game where everyone stands around analyzing like it was a World Cup final

---

### Reddit (Tactical)

**Why:** r/soccer, r/5aside, r/pickupsoccer — exactly our people. But Reddit hates promo.

**Strategy:**
- Be a community member first. Comment genuinely for 2-3 weeks.
- When someone asks "how do you pick fair teams?" — mention naturally
- r/SideProject: "I built an app for my pickup group" — tell the story
- Never lead with the app. Lead with the experience.

## Growth Tactics (Zero Budget)

### 1. Be a Culture Page First

Follow and engage with 30-50 accounts in the pickup football world:
- 5-a-side venues and leagues
- Football culture/meme pages
- Local pickup group accounts
- Football content creators who actually play casual

Comment genuinely. "This is literally our group" — not "check out our app!"

### 2. Hashtag Strategy

**Core (every post):** #pickupfootball #5aside #sundayleague

**Reach (rotate 5-7 per post):**
- #footballlife #footballculture #casualfootball #pitchlife
- #7aside #kickabout #footballmemes #soccerlife
- #weekendfootball #tuesdayfootball #thursdayfootball

**Niche (on relevant content):**
- #pickupsoccer #fairteams #footballbanter #groupchat

**Product posts only:** #picknkick #footballapp #teamdraft

**Rule:** 8-12 hashtags. Never more than 15. Mix big and small.

### 3. Cross-Platform Repurposing

Every idea gets 3 lives:
1. **Instagram** carousel or Reel
2. **TikTok** short video
3. **X** one-liner or thread

The "Starting XI" carousel becomes a TikTok with a trending sound becomes a Twitter thread.

### 4. Make People Tag Their Group

The best content makes people think "I need to send this to the group chat." Design every post around that reaction:
- "Tag the organizer of your group — they deserve it"
- "Send this to the guy who always shows up late"
- "Your group's captain needs to see this"

Don't write "tag someone" — make the content so specific they tag people on their own.

### 5. Micro-Creators and Venues

Target: football culture accounts with 1K-50K followers. People who actually play pickup.

- 5-a-side venue accounts are gold — they have exactly our audience
- Offer to feature their venue or group
- Mutual benefit: we get exposure, they get content about their community

### 6. User-Generated Content

Once real users exist:
- Encourage sharing draft results with @picknkick tag
- Reshare to Stories (free content + social proof)
- "Show us your pitch" — photos of where people play worldwide
- "What does your group call a [football thing]?" — regional slang is engagement gold

## Metrics

### What Actually Matters

Forget follower count. Track:
- **Saves** — people want to come back to it
- **Shares** — people sent it to their group chat
- **Profile visits** — content made them curious who we are
- **Link clicks** — they went to picknkick.com

Likes and comments are vanity. Saves and shares are signal.

### Targets

| Metric | Month 1 | Month 3 | Month 6 |

## What NOT to Do

1. **Don't make every post about the app.** Max 1 in 6. The page is about pickup football, not PickNKick.
2. **Don't buy followers.** Tanks engagement. Algorithm punishes you.
3. **Don't beg for engagement.** "LIKE if you agree" is cringe. Make content worth engaging with.
4. **Don't post corporate content.** No "We're excited to announce..." — nobody cares.
5. **Don't spread too thin.** Instagram + TikTok + X is enough.
6. **Don't post just to post.** Skip a day before posting filler. Quality > consistency.
7. **Don't ignore comments.** Reply to everything in the first few months. Be a person, not a brand.

## The Big Picture

This Instagram page should feel like a **pickup football community** that happens to have an app. If we stripped PickNKick out of the bio, the content should still make sense and still get followers.

The strategy:
1. **Attract** — relatable pickup football content brings people in
2. **Relate** — they follow because "this page gets my life"
3. **Solve** — when teams come up (organically, not forced), we have the answer
4. **Convert** — they try picknkick.com because they already trust the page

The app is the quiet closer. The content is the relationship builder.
